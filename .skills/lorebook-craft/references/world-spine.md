# World Spine

Load for a new substantial world before planning entries. The spine makes many entries cohere around one logic instead of floating separately.

## Governing logic

Choose the smallest rule set that makes many parts of the world cohere. Three common forms:

- **Translation law:** one register consistently re-expressed through another, such as ritual as bureaucracy or reputation as currency.
- **Material law:** one physical, magical, economic, or institutional rule with broad consequences.
- **Pressure law:** one recurring constraint — scarcity, surveillance, debt, status, seasonal danger, inheritance — that shapes everyday choices.

State the logic in one or two sentences, then test it against unrelated elements: a location, institution, livelihood, punishment, relationship, and piece of slang. If it cannot generate useful consequences, broaden or replace it.

Do not force a universal law onto a realistic setting that already has enough coherence. In such cases use a real referent and identify the specific local pressures that matter to play.

## Topology

Topology is how characters move through power and space, not merely a map. The organizing axis may be physical layers or districts, center and periphery, public and private thresholds, social class or institutional rank, legal versus illicit access, or time and season.

Define only the zones needed for play. For each important boundary, record:

- what separates the sides;
- who may cross;
- the cost or condition of crossing;
- what leaks across anyway;
- how rules or perceptions change after crossing.

At least one boundary should produce a recurring scene engine.

## Forces and named tensions

For each important faction, institution, community, or antagonist-as-force, record:

- want;
- resource;
- method;
- limit;
- dependency;
- public story;
- private contradiction.

Name tensions precisely. Prefer dependency, contested legitimacy, incompatible definitions, unequal exchange, mutual misreading, or shared goals pursued by incompatible means over generic "they are enemies." Leave major tensions unresolved unless the brief requires a settled history.

Scale is not interaction. A primary antagonist or hostile system needs something it wants that can be engaged: the player should be able to offer, deny, expose, bargain around, or misdirect it.

## Consequence chain

Connect mechanics to lived texture:

Rule or pressure -> institutional response -> daily adaptation -> sensory or social texture -> conflict opportunity.

A curfew matters because it changes work shifts, routes, prices, family routines, rumors, and who can plausibly meet whom.

## Anchoring

Each important entry connects to at least two other canon elements:

- one structural anchor to a rule, boundary, institution, or force;
- one relational anchor to a dependency, conflict, history, or consequence.

"The guild controls the bridge" is weaker than "the guild's debt monopoly makes bridge passage the moment every traveler becomes legible to it."

## Open pressure

Leave each major area with a crack: an unresolved cost, false public story, unstable boundary, disputed history, or approaching deadline. The crack invites play without predetermining the answer.

## Optional lenses

Choose no more than one or two. A lens changes emphasis; it is not mandatory structure.

**Iceberg** — uncertainty, dread, or historical depth depends on what remains unstated. Decide the hidden structure privately, expose consequences before explanations, leave some boundaries unresolved. Do not withhold everything: the player still needs reliable handles for action.

**Negative space** — absence shapes the present: a vanished institution, forbidden practice, missing generation, abandoned district, broken succession. Define who benefits from the absence, show how routines grew around the gap, make factions disagree over filling or preserving it.

**Conflict engine** — politics, war, court intrigue, or faction choice drives play. Build the force web before detailed geography, derive neutral ground and crossing costs from the conflict, give each force internal disagreement and at least one dependency on a rival.

**Real referent** — a real place, era, industry, or institution borrows coherence. The referent is useful when it creates pressure: labor patterns, housing, transport, class, climate, regulation. A famous name used only as decoration adds little. Verify facts that materially affect canon; distinguish verified reality from fictional alteration; avoid stereotypes and do not assume the model's training memory is accurate.

## Build order

1. governing logic;
2. topology;
3. forces and tensions;
4. consequence chains;
5. entry graph (see entry-plan);
6. canon ledger;
7. consistency pass (see consistency).

If the result feels flat, diagnose the missing layer instead of adding more nouns.
