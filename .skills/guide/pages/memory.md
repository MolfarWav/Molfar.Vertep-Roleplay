# Which memory for what

Roleplay has several things that "remember". They differ in who keeps them, what they hold and how long they last. If a character forgets something, this page tells you which one to reach for.

## The kinds

**Chat history.** The messages themselves. The model sees as many recent ones as its window allows, except that a reply spoken by one character can leave out the scenes that character was not in ([[litopys]]).

**Dashboard** ([[dashboard]]). Per chat, the *current* state of the scene: who is present, feelings, outfits, open quests, what each character knows. It changes with every reply.

**Litopys** ([[litopys]]). Per chat, the *past* of the story: a chapter per finished scene and a list of facts. This is what a long chat leans on after older messages leave the prompt.

**The card's Memory tab.** Per character, across chats: the habits you keep on the card, and "How they changed" (below). It is not part of the card text and never appears in an exported card. The facts of a story are not kept here: Litopys keeps them, per chat, and a chat that continues another brings them as its backstory. (Older versions had a "Stories" block here, where a finished chat was imported into the character's memory. It is gone: a story saved there earlier stays in the file but is no longer sent in any prompt. A persona's and a saved world's Memory still have their stories.)

**How they changed.** A living portrait of the character, kept in the same tab and written in blocks like the card's own text, named by the Profile tab's sections: Background, Relationships, Personality, Behaviors, Goals, Trauma, Sexuality, Other (not the name or looks, which hardly ever change). A block either updates what the card says about that part ("Personality (now): she no longer hides what she feels...") or adds something new that came out in the story (a relationship that grew, a secret, a skill, a piece of the past). The "Character growth" agent (Agents board, "Start from a ready agent") writes one block now and then; how often is its "Every Nth reply", and you can write your own. A proposed block waits under "Waiting for your yes": Accept, Edit or Delete. When it updates a block you already accepted, you see both texts side by side, and accepting keeps the old one so you can go back. Accepted blocks that are on go into the chats they act in, as one part that says that where it differs from the card's description and personality, this is more recent and wins; the card itself is never changed, so a Profile that rebuilds the description cannot erase it. (The card's own text stays in the prompt, because a Profile card's description is compiled text that cannot be cut apart reliably.) You set how many tokens the portrait may use (100 to 8000, 1500 by default): a bigger room means a fuller portrait and costs tokens in every reply; when there are more blocks than room, the newest are sent and the tab says how many are left out. The part has its own switch on the Prompt board, so one chat can ignore it, and a master switch in the tab turns it off everywhere. The tab has a button **Update from the story**: it reads the chat chosen at the top of the block, or the newest chat with this character when all chats are shown (it names the chat under the button) in one bigger look and proposes up to four blocks at once, to accept or dismiss like the agent's; nothing is used before you accept. A switch, **Also remind near the newest message**, repeats the two newest blocks at the end of the prompt, because models weigh late text more, so the portrait wins over the card more often; it costs about 200 tokens more per reply and is off by default. In a chat, the strip of what the chat carries shows the portrait as a part of its own ("Portrait of ..."), with its tokens, its switch and what is sent. In a group chat the portrait works too. When the members answer one at a time ("swap"), only the member who is about to speak brings their portrait. When one voice answers for everyone ("narrator") or every member's card is in the prompt ("append"), every member who has accepted blocks brings theirs under their own name, and they share one room: the biggest of the members' rooms, shared out member by member, newest blocks first. The button works from a group chat the card is in and writes blocks about this card only. The ready agent Character growth is for a chat with one character; an agent of your own names the member in its "who" input.

**When the story may have changed a character.** While you play, the app watches for three signs without asking any model: a new turning point of the story, a character whose relation to you moved to another class (say from a stranger to a friend), and a pivotal event in the Dashboard (at most once in eight replies). When it sees one, a blue notice says what happened and offers **Update portrait** or **Not now**. Only the button asks a model: it does what "Update from the story" does here, and the blocks it proposes wait under "Waiting for your yes" as usual. In a group the notice is about the member the story names; in a chat of one card, about that card. It tells once per sign, and says nothing about what was already in a chat when you first opened it. It follows the switch for portrait notices in the Library's Notices settings.

**Where a block acts.** A block an agent or "Update from the story" wrote is bound to the story it came from: it acts in that chat and in the chats that continue it (the chat links), and not in another story of the same character, so a fresh chat does not start with what happened elsewhere. A block you write yourself acts in every chat of the character. Under each accepted block a button says where it acts now ("This story", "This story and 2 more chats", "Every chat of Aria"); press it, or choose "Where it acts" in the block's menu, to switch between "Only this story" and "Every chat" and to switch the block on or off chat by chat. A block you wrote cannot be bound to a story (it has no chat). In the chat itself, the **Carries** panel (the row "How the character changed") has a fold "Growth blocks in this chat" with one switch per block and why it is in or out; it changes only that chat. Blocks an agent wrote before stories existed are bound to the chat they came from the first time the tab or a chat reads them, so they no longer reach a new chat; one whose chat was deleted keeps acting in every chat, and "Where it acts" turns any of them back to "Every chat".

**One chat at a time.** The same character can live different stories in different chats, so a card with two or more chats has a **Chat** picker at the top of the block. "All chats" (the default) lists every block; each chat in the list shows its title, when it was last played, how many messages it has, the start of its newest message and how many blocks act in it, which tells chats with the same title apart (a new chat is named after the card). The search box at the top of the list finds a chat by its title, a word of its newest message, a date ("27 Nov") or a length ("40 msgs"); several words can come in any order. Pick a chat and the block shows only what goes into that chat: the waiting proposals from it, the blocks of its story and the ones for every chat. A line says how many are hidden, with **Show all chats**; the token line becomes "This chat gets ... tokens", and Update from the story reads that chat. With all chats shown, the token line adds up every block and says that a chat gets only its own. The tab remembers the chat you picked for each card. When several chats share a title, a block's "From the chat" line also gives the date it was written. Character growth and Update from the story see only the blocks of the chat they work in, so a change in one story is not taken as already written in another.

**A card that voices several characters.** A narrator card (or any card whose Soul tab lists two or more characters) keeps a portrait of each character, not one for the card. Each block says who it is about: the Memory tab shows a heading per character (the blocks written before this, with no name, stay under the card's own name), the add and edit forms have an "About" list, and the prompt carries every character's blocks under their own name. Character growth and the "Update from the story" button name the character themselves, from the Soul tab's list; a block that names none of them, or two, is not stored. A new block about one character never proposes to replace another's.

**Lorebooks.** Reference entries that join the prompt when their keywords appear, or always for a few. They hold the world's rules, places, people. Keys in Ukrainian and Russian match every form of a word, and a name written with a capital in a key also finds the same name in the other alphabet: the key *Kael* fires on «Каел» and «Каелу» in the chat, and the key «Каел» on *Kael*. A name that sounds different («Кейл») does not match, and a lowercase key such as *power* never crosses alphabets (translate the book for that). The book's **Word forms** switch, on by default, controls both. Link a book to a card or group, or mark it Global to reach every chat. A chat also gets its own world book written by the World DM ([[world-dm]]). An entry set to **By meaning** fires when its sense is close to the recent messages, even without a key. With a local embeddings model the engine compares the entries of a book with each other: an entry fires when it stands out from the rest for this message, up to 10 entries per book. A book with fewer than 4 such entries uses a fixed closeness. An entry with secondary keys still needs them. If you change the embeddings model, the entries are made again by themselves on the next reply or two.

**Folders in a lorebook.** A big book is easier to read in folders. Press **New folder** beside **Add entry** to make one (it starts empty), then open an entry and use **Folder**, or press **Select several** and **Move to folder…**. Once a book has a folder, the **Entries** tab becomes a tree: folders first (each shows how many entries it holds and their tokens), then the entries with no folder. A folder's menu can add an entry there, make a folder inside it (up to three levels; or type `Places/Taverns` as a name), switch all its entries on or off (the ones below too), rename the folder or give it a colour, or remove it (its entries and the folders inside it move up one level). On a computer you can also drag an entry onto a folder (or onto **No folder**); a ticked entry drags every ticked one. On a phone use **Move to folder…**. Search and the filter chips open the folders that have a match. Folders only tidy the list: they do not change when an entry fires, and they are not the **Group** setting (of one group only one entry fires). An entry opens from the left over the whole book on a computer (the Library drawer goes full screen while it is open, and back when you close it): the text on the left, tall, to read and edit, and when it appears, keys, folder and the rest on the right. On a phone it is a full page.

**Secrets in a lorebook.** Some lore should reach only the characters who know it: the stranger who is really the king, the old dagger that is really the Soul-eater. Open the entry and turn on **Secret**: write who knows it from the start, **What the others see** (the version everyone else lives with, for example "an old dagger with strange runes"; leave it empty and the entry stays out for them), and **They learn it when they hear**: words that give the truth away. A reply spoken by a character who does not know gets the cover text; one who knows gets the truth. Anyone who is in the scene (by the Dashboard) when one of those words is said learns it from then on. A narrator card, which voices everyone, gets the truth with a note saying who knows it. You can also write **Or they learn it when this happens**: one short sentence such as "Jane is made to eat a clam". The Dashboard's sensor then looks for it after each reply (one more line in its prompt, no extra call, and not in fast mode) and, when it clearly took place, everyone in the scene learns the truth. A model judges it, so small free ones may miss it; the words above are the sure way. For something a character does not know about themself yet (a craving, a fear), press **Start from a hidden trait** in the Secret fold: nobody knows it at the start and the entry is always on, but the character only gets it after the word is heard or the event happens, and then for the rest of that chat. A new chat starts without it. Secret entries show a lock in the entry list. Who knows in a given chat is shown and changed in the chat's **Fired** panel (below, "What fired in this chat").

**Data Bank** (Tools). Text documents you upload (text formats only, up to 2 MB each). On every message the most relevant passages are added: found by words first, and with an embeddings model set up also ordered by meaning (the card **Data Bank by meaning** on the same screen: one press, or the engine does it by itself once embedding works). Use it for long reference material, not for the story itself. A **book** is a Data Bank file made from a story: in Library > Litopys, **Save as a book** copies the chapters, arcs and facts of a chat into a shelf file that other chats can read. Pick the chats that may read it (the chats that continue this one are chosen for you). It is a copy, not a link: later changes to the chat do not reach it, and **Save a new version** keeps the old one. A character only gets passages they could know: a fact keeps its "known by" list, a chapter its cast. In the Data Bank tab, **passages** lets you fix or delete a passage and change the readers; the Memory view counts the passages kept out.

**Author's note** (chat header, "Note"). Text you write for this chat only, placed at a depth and role you choose. Use it to steer: a style hint, a plot push.

**Stage directions** (the message box). Text you put in `[[double brackets]]` inside your own message is cut out of your words and sent to the model as an out-of-character instruction, high in priority, for the reply to that message only (and its swipes and regenerations); it is not remembered by anything. Under your message it shows as a muted chip with an x to take it off (no x while a reply is being written). The "+" menu's **Stage direction** types the brackets for you, and a line under the box counts the directions in your draft. A message that holds only directions adds no turn of yours to the story: the model gets the instruction after the last reply and writes the next one. Only the newest message's directions go with a request; an older one does not repeat. By default the instruction goes at the end of the request as a system message; a preset's **Stage directions role** (Presets > Shaping) can make it a user message for models that ignore system messages there, and the chat's prompt board (Canvas > Prompt, row **Stage directions**) switches it off for that chat. Single brackets `[like this]` are not directions. **Continue from someone's view:** when the persona and the others have gone different ways, the "+" menu's **Continue from someone's view** lists the people the Dashboard knows (the ones in the scene first, marked "here") and the chat's characters; picking one sends a direction alone, "Continue the story from <name>'s point of view", and the next reply follows them. "Someone else..." puts the direction in the box for you to type a name into. It needs the Stage directions row of the prompt board to be on.
 **Skip time:** the "+" menu's **Skip time...** opens a small window: a number, a unit (minutes, hours, days, weeks, months, years) and an optional note ("until the morning", "the train arrives"). With a number, the note is extra detail; with no number, the note alone says how long. **Skip time** sends one direction alone ("Time skip: 3 days. ..."): the next reply jumps over the gap and goes on from its end, and the Dashboard clock follows what the reply says. Nothing is written in the chat as your message.

**Soul** ([[soul]]). Not a memory of events but how a character reacts; it never changes during play.

## Which to use

- "She should remember last night's fight": Dashboard handles it short-term; Litopys keeps it long-term. Add a pinned fact if it must never be lost.
- "She should remember the previous story in a new chat": link the chats on the Canvas so the earlier chat brings its story as backstory ([[canvas]], [[long-chats]]).
- "The world has a rule or a place": a lorebook.
- "Only some characters may know what this thing or person really is": a secret lorebook entry with a cover text.
- "I want the next replies to go this way": author's note, or the "Story, move" button. "Only this one reply, and it is not me speaking": a stage direction in `[[double brackets]]`.
- "I have a long document": Data Bank.

## Why did she know that?

Open **Canvas > Prompt** ([[canvas]]) and switch the board from **Parts** to **Memory**. It shows what memory put into the last reply, and what it left out and why: the facts that went in (with their rank and who knows them), the ones kept out because the speaker does not know them, the ones that did not fit the memory size, the chapters, what the Data Bank found, and anything the checks after the reply flagged. People appear with their id from the chat's list of people, so "Sina" and "Sina Rose" are told apart. **Copy report** puts the same thing in your clipboard as plain text: paste it to whoever helps you debug the memory. The row of buttons at the top goes back through the last five replies (the Data Bank hits go with them); the checks after the reply are kept for the newest one only. A fold named **Worker: left out of the last scene** lists the facts the chapter worker did not keep and why: a near copy of a fact it already had, only what the card says, more than eight in one scene, a fact that is already gone, or the same proposal already waiting. The **Checked after the reply** fold names the facts the Dashboard sensor could not keep (about your own character, about someone who is not in the scene, an important event with no quote), not only how many. The summary also says **Ranked by meaning: yes** with how many of the candidates had a vector, or **no** and why (the chat has no vectors yet, the last call to the embeddings model failed, or this reply was not embedded). That tells "the answer was never in memory" from "the search by meaning was off". In the chat, the panel "What this chat carries" has a **Why?** button on the Litopys row that opens the **Fired** panel on its Litopys tab.

## What fired in this chat

The **Fired** button (lightning) in the chat header opens one panel for the last reply, with tabs by source: **Lorebooks** (blue), **Litopys** (violet), **Data Bank** (teal) and **Vectors** (green). The switch at the top picks **Last reply** or **Whole chat**. For the last reply, Lorebooks groups the entries by the way they got in: by meaning (with a closeness bar, the closest first), by key (which key hit), held over from before (sticky), always on (not counted, they go into every reply), then **Held back** (a delay, a cooldown, a chance, a group, or secondary keys that failed, also for an entry that was close in meaning) and **Over the budget**. For the whole chat, each lorebook entry, Litopys fact or chapter and Data Bank passage shows "N of M replies", how it got in and when it last went in. Under the lorebook list, **Never fired** names, book by book, the enabled entries that went into none of the counted replies (always-on entries are left out): handy for tidying a book, since a key that never matches is a key to fix or an entry to drop. When the log is full it says the list is only a hint. The count is approximate: an entry that stays in the scan window counts in every reply that carried it; a swipe replaces the reply before it; a preview adds nothing; nothing is counted from before the update. In a story of many chats, the Litopys tab also has **Far backstory** (the condensed older chats): a fold for the last reply, and for the whole chat how many replies carried it. The Vectors tab shows which memory has vectors (the chat's record, the Data Bank and the lorebook entries set to "By meaning", book by book) and which source used them in the last reply. Lorebook vectors have no button: they are made while the next reply is prepared. A secret entry that went in shows how: **Cover shown** (the speaker does not know it), **Known to the speaker**, or **Truth, marked for the narrator**; one held back because the speaker does not know it and it has no cover is listed under Held back. When the chat's books have secret entries, the fold **Secrets in this chat** lists, per entry, who knows it and how (from the start, heard a word, added by you): remove a name with its x, or add one with **Add who knows** (the names of the latest scene are suggested). **Copy report** gives the same as plain text. The Lorebooks page, section Active, still checks what *would* fire for the next message, by keys only.

## Costs

All of these take room in the prompt. The Canvas Prompt board shows each part with its token count and lets you switch parts off for one chat. On a small model, lower Litopys's "Memory size", keep the habits room small, and prefer a few short lorebook entries. A chat that has grown long costs more with every reply: [[long-chats]] says what trims it and how to continue the story in a fresh chat. The Canvas page, [[canvas]], explains the boards.
