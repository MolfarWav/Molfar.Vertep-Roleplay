# Character Depth Method

Use when the request is about a serious character, a flat or monolithic lead, a long arc, or an ensemble whose members blur together. Skip for one-shot utility NPCs.

## Causal spine

Build five outputs. Do not force a clinical framework or a traumatic origin.

### 1. Formative pressure

One or two conditions that trained the character's current strategies. Loss, repeated humiliation, responsibility, privilege, institutional pressure, family culture, professional success, or an ordinary habit reinforced for years. A useful pressure: implies consequences, explains at least two present behaviors, has a present residue. If no single origin exists, use a pattern of reinforcement. Do not manufacture trauma.

### 2. Wants, fears, beliefs, obligations

Four separate forces:

- **Want:** what they actively pursue. One long-arc want and one scene-level want.
- **Fear:** what outcome they organize behavior to prevent.
- **Belief:** what they think is true about people, the world, or themselves.
- **Obligation:** what they must do even when it conflicts with desire.

Important goals need costs, competition, or mutually exclusive outcomes. One unconstrained goal predicts behavior.

### 3. Protective strategies

Observable behavior that preserves safety, status, control, identity, or belonging. Families: avoidance, vigilance, control, performance, appeasement, humor, competence, caretaking, information asymmetry, provocation, withdrawal, over-preparation, ritual, counter-phobic pursuit.

For each important strategy record: trigger, immediate benefit, longer-term cost, visible form, what escalates or relaxes it. Avoid labels like "has trust issues" — name the playable behavior.

### 4. Mask and registers

The public adaptation, not necessarily a lie. Registers only as large as the role needs: public/routine, competent/engaged, defensive/threatened, unguarded/off-duty, plus intimate, grieving, commanding, playful, ceremonial, professional when relevant. For each register: sentence shape, vocabulary, silence, humor, address, posture, initiative, what they refuse to discuss. One register is not the permanent voice.

### 5. Inner contradiction

Two genuine parts of the character in conflict: want versus strategy, belief versus evidence, obligation versus desire, self-image versus behavior, two incompatible loyalties. A useful contradiction generates choices without external plot, connects to the formative pressure, has concrete flashpoints, cannot be solved by one reassuring conversation, and may never resolve.

## Causal-chain check

Complete this sentence before writing prose:

> Because of [formative pressure], the character came to believe or fear [X], wants or owes [Y], and protects themselves through [strategies]. This produces the public mask [Z] and breaks differently under [triggers]. The conflict between [two forces] drives present choices and can change only when [evidence or cost].

Repair the cause rather than adding another trait.

## Operationalize into play

### Pressure progression

Defenses have a baseline, a pressured state, and a limit state. For each: speech and body changes, the action taken, what they still want, how recovery happens, what persists. No instant reset.

### Body grammar

A small set of distinctive actions linked to purpose or history. Prefer actions that vary with context over tics repeated every reply. A gesture is useful when its meaning changes under pressure.

### Micro-agenda

A concrete want in the scene that exists without the player, producing actions between dialogue beats. Future scenes generate new micro-agendas from stable goals and current state.

### Knowledge boundaries

Separate: what they know and admit; what they know but conceal; what they misread or rationalize; what they cannot know. Self-deception shapes choices without narrator announcements.

### Change conditions

Development uses evidence, cost, and reversibility: what repeated evidence challenges the belief, what they risk by changing, what old strategy tempts, what setback could restore it, what new behavior would prove change. No fixed turn count.

### Load-bearing relationships

For each formative or active relationship, record what it supplies: standard, refuge, rivalry, obligation, temptation, access, threat, unresolved comparison. A reachable backstory figure becomes an escape hatch if every hard choice is delegated to them. Fix with limits, competing incentives, partial knowledge, distance, or changed circumstances. Removal is one option, never the default.

### Antagonist extension

An antagonist needs an engageable want: something the player can offer, deny, expose, bargain around, or complicate. Power without leverage produces spectacle, not interaction.

## Audit

For an existing flat character, score each dimension `strong`, `thin`, `contradictory`, or `missing` with evidence: causality, agency, protective behavior, voice and registers, contradiction and change, relationships, integration. For each issue, report the smallest repair and where it goes.

Preflight probes: neutral scene, competence pressure, mild trigger, high pressure, contradiction flashpoint, recovery next scene, displacement outside the opening, relationship contrast, antagonist leverage. Predict behavior before testing.

## Ensembles

Build the relational system before individuals:

1. **Functions:** who stabilizes, provokes, witnesses, bridges, withholds, translates, disrupts. Current functions, not permanent archetypes.
2. **Pair dynamics:** affinity, friction, leverage, shared history, the behavior each evokes in the other.
3. **Third-person effect:** how a third person changes a pair. Often more revealing than the pair alone.
4. **Trigger chains:** two or three plausible sequences where one behavior changes another, which changes a third. Probabilistic, not deterministic.
5. **Information asymmetry:** who knows, suspects, misreads, hides what.
6. **Group mask:** the collective story, what it conceals, when it cracks.
7. **External pressure:** what keeps the cast interacting — resource, institution, deadline, location, rival, obligation.

Each recurring member needs: one current want not reducible to the player, one protective strategy, one distinctive voice or silence pattern, one relationship-specific behavior, one condition that makes them act against their usual function. Do not give every member the full solo method.

Voice separation through sentence shape, turn openings, address, humor, precision, interruption, silence. Vocabulary lists alone are weak. The test: remove labels and check whether the likely speaker remains identifiable.

Preflight: neutral group scene, one member absent, two members disagree, player focuses on one, a pressure event shifts functions. Flag voice convergence, silent-member disappearance, automatic consensus, one-character domination, relationships existing only through the player.
